Why "Nitya" Seva?

Nitya means eternal, daily. The Lord's service never takes a day off. The lamps are lit, the bhoga is cooked, the doors open to every guest, whether it is Janmashtami or an ordinary Tuesday.

A one-time gift is a beautiful offering. A regular offering is a relationship: it lets the temple plan, care for devotees, and say "yes" to every soul who walks in looking for Krishna.

Lives touched

How a seeker becomes a devotee

Every step of this journey happens at the temple, and every step is sustained by sevaks like you.

  1. 1

    The first visit

    A student from the neighbourhood walks in, curious. Someone greets them warmly and offers prasadam.

  2. 2

    The first kirtan

    They sit at the back, hesitant. By the end, they are clapping and singing the holy names.

  3. 3

    Hearing the Gita

    Weekly classes begin answering questions they never knew how to ask: Who am I? Why am I here?

  4. 4

    Taking up chanting

    Japa beads in hand, they begin each day with Krishna. Their life slowly fills with peace and purpose.

  5. 5

    Serving others

    Now they welcome the next newcomer at the door. The circle of devotion grows.

Questions sevaks often ask

Do I pay on this website?

No. This form only lets us know you'd like to serve. A devotee will contact you personally and share the ways to make your offering.

Can I change, pause or stop my monthly seva?

Of course. Seva is an offering of love, never an obligation. Just let us know anytime.

Will I receive a receipt?

Yes, you will receive a receipt for every offering. You can ask the devotee who contacts you about tax-exemption eligibility.

Can I offer seva on a birthday, anniversary or in someone's memory?

Yes. Choose the occasion in the form and we will make sure a special offering is made to the Lord on your behalf.

Can I serve in other ways too?

Absolutely. Cooking, decorating, kirtan, festival help, book distribution: there's a place for everyone. Mention it in your message and we'll connect you.
